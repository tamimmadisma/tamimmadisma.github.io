Learning Outside
My Field

Today I accompanied the CEO of PT Cahaya Segara Indonesia to UI and BRIN for research related to corrosion and material inspection for ships.

The technical side was completely outside my field—metallurgy, SEM/EDX, material samples, and even water samples. I didn't understand everything, but I found it interesting seeing how the research was being figured out.

One of the professors working with PT Cahaya Segara Indonesia helped discuss the research with UI researchers, especially around what could be investigated and what facilities were needed. We eventually went to BRIN as well because some of the research required capabilities beyond what was available at UI.

You don't always need to know the answer. Sometimes you just need to know who to ask.

As an ITB graduate, I was honestly a little jealous seeing the facilities and research capabilities at UI. 😂 But it also reminded me that ITB gave me a different kind of experience through its startup incubator and entrepreneurship ecosystem.

What stayed with me most was seeing how a real business problem can require different people, expertise, and institutions to come together.

What I learned today

When you don't have the answer, find the people and resources that can help you get there.

Stay curious. Ask better questions. Keep learning outside your field.

Maybe “I Can't” Was Too Easy

Today was one of those days where several things seemed to happen at once.

A business partner from the Philippines reached out about an upcoming M COUNTDOWN live voting period. They were looking for around 3,000 votes, while with my current manpower, I could realistically handle around 1,500.

Rather than immediately turning the request down, I decided to see if I could find a way to increase my capacity.

I started reaching out to potential new employees through Threads. This wasn't easy to do alongside everything else. I was recruiting while simultaneously running my digital business, managing my existing team, handling ongoing work, and making sure everything continued moving.

I reached out to around 20 people.

In the end, only one person was able to handle the programming and workflow required.

It wasn't the number I initially hoped for, but it was still progress. It also reminded me that building a team isn't simply about finding more people. It's about finding the right people who can actually contribute.

“Instead of asking, ‘Do I have enough people?’ I started asking, ‘Can I find the people I need?’”

The same day also brought some encouraging developments in my career. An opportunity I had been pursuing moved forward, while another professional opportunity was progressing through its assessment stage.

Having different things develop at the same time made the day feel significant. It was a reminder that the work I've been putting into my business, my skills, and my career is gradually creating new possibilities.

What I learned today

Maybe I don't need to have enough before I begin.

Sometimes, I just need to begin — and figure out what “enough” looks like along the way.

Maybe “I can't” was too easy.
